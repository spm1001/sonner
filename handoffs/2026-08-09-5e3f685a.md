## For Claudes to come

Three classes of live Claude session exist on this estate, and a messaging tool must treat them differently. Terminal sessions on personal Max billing bind an inbox socket and are fully ringable. Work-billed Vertex sessions (`claudev`/`claudefv`) write complete registry records but the `messagingSocketPath` field is simply absent — they are deaf by provider gating, they were the *majority* of live sessions when measured (5 of 8), and no amount of socket-sweeping will find them. iOS remote-control sessions (the `claude remote-control` daemons under user systemd are Guéridon's substrate) are socketed and persistent: a peer message wakes them when idle, but one arriving mid-task queues and never gets a turn once the task completes — delivered is not read.

Two design principles fell out of watching this fail live. First: **env vars are hints; the uid is ground truth** — `$HOME`, `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` and their kin describe the caller's world-view, not the machine, and any discovery keyed solely on them goes blind exactly when a harness rewrites the environment. Second: **never present an address that cannot receive** — a receiver handed `script:sonner-3d` as a from-address did its honest best, guessed from the roster, and delivered its reply to an unrelated session whose tmux window happened to be named `sonner-infra-signboard`. The fix was both mechanical (a socketed caller's real name in `from`, an explicit do-not-reply footer otherwise) and nominal (spawned sessions are `rung-<repo>` now, so the tool's own children stop masquerading as the tool).

Operationally: `tmux kill-session` does not kill the claude inside it — the child survives reparented to systemd. Kill by registry pid; claude cleans its own record on SIGTERM.
